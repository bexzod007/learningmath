Evaluating Expressions

"Evaluating" an expression simply means calculating its final numerical value after substituting real numbers in place of the secret letters.

1. The 3-Step Master Protocol

  1. Plug in with Parentheses: Replace every variable with its assigned number inside parentheses (). This keeps negative signs and operations safe!
  2. Follow PEMDAS strictly:
    • Parentheses first
    • Exponents next
    • Multiplication and Division (left to right)
    • Addition and Subtraction (left to right)
  3. Simplify: Calculate one line at a time until you reach one single final number.

2. Step-by-Step Walkthrough (Basic)

Problem: Evaluate 5x - 4 when x = 3.

  • Step 1 (Substitute): Replace x with (3) → 5(3) - 4
  • Step 2 (Multiply): 5 × 3 = 15 → 15 - 4
  • Step 3 (Subtract): 15 - 4 = 11
  • Final Answer: 11

3. Step-by-Step Walkthrough (Advanced with Exponents & Negatives)

Problem: Evaluate 3x² - 2x + 5 when x = -2.

  • Step 1 (Substitute carefully using parentheses):
    3(-2)² - 2(-2) + 5
  • Step 2 (Exponents first):
    Calculate (-2)² = (-2) × (-2) = +4
    Expression becomes: 3(4) - 2(-2) + 5
  • Step 3 (Multiplication left to right):
    First part: 3(4) = 12
    Second part: -2(-2) = +4 (Negative times negative equals positive!)
    Expression becomes: 12 + 4 + 5
  • Step 4 (Add left to right):
    12 + 4 = 16
    16 + 5 = 21
  • Final Answer: 21

Pro Tip on Negative Numbers: Always write parentheses around negative numbers when substituting! (-3)² equals +9, but writing -3² without parentheses can lead to the wrong answer (-9).
