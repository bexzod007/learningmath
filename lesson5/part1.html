What is an Expression?

In English, we combine words to form a phrase like "a fast red car." It gives you an idea, but it is not a complete sentence because it lacks an action verb.

In mathematics, an expression is the exact same thing: a mathematical phrase. It combines numbers, letters, and operations, but it does not have an equals sign (=).

1. The 3 Main Ingredients

  • Variables (The Secret Boxes): A letter (such as x, y, a, or n) that represents an unknown number or a value that can change. Think of it as a gift box—you don't know what's inside until you open it.
  • Constants (The Anchors): A fixed number that never changes (like 5, -12, or ½). A 5 is always worth 5.
  • Operations (The Action Signs): Symbols like addition (+), subtraction (-), multiplication (×), and division (÷) that tell you what to do with the numbers and variables.

2. Expression vs. Equation (The Golden Difference)

Students often mix these two up. Here is the easiest way to remember:

Feature Algebraic Expression Algebraic Equation
Definition A math phrase (no result yet) A complete math sentence (two equal sides)
Equals Sign? NO equals sign (=) YES, always has an equals sign (=)
Example 3x + 4 3x + 4 = 19
Goal Simplify or Evaluate Solve for the variable

3. How Multiplication is Hidden in Algebra

In basic math, you wrote multiplication like this: 3 × x. But in algebra, the symbol × looks too much like the letter x! To prevent confusion, mathematicians write multiplication in 3 cleaner ways:

  1. Direct Attachment: 3x (means 3 times x)
  2. Parentheses: 3(x) or (3)(x)
  3. Dot Symbol: 3 · x

Teacher's Tip: Whenever you see a number right next to a letter without any symbol between them (like 7y or 10ab), it always means multiplication!
