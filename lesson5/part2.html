Terms

If an expression is like a train, then terms are the individual train cars connected together. Understanding terms is the key to simplifying complex algebra problems later on.

1. How to Count Terms

Terms are separated ONLY by plus (+) or minus (-) signs. Multiplication and division glue numbers and letters together into a single term.

Example 1: Look at 4x + 7

  • This expression has 2 terms: 4x and 7.

Example 2: Look at 5x² - 3xy + 8y - 12

  • This expression has 4 terms: 5x², -3xy, 8y, and -12.

CRITICAL RULE: The Sign Belongs to the Term!
The sign directly in front of a term belongs to that term like a backpack. In 8x - 5, the second term is NOT 5—it is -5!

2. Anatomy of a Term

A single term can be built in three different ways:

  • A Single Number (Constant Term): e.g., +9, -4
  • A Single Letter (Variable Term): e.g., x, a²
  • A Combination of Numbers and Letters: e.g., -6x²y

3. Like Terms vs. Unlike Terms

To combine terms in algebra, they must be "like terms." Think of it as sorting fruit: apples go with apples, oranges with oranges.

Type Condition Examples Can you combine them?
Like Terms Same variable(s) raised to the exact same exponent 3x and 8x
5y² and -2y²		 YES!
(3x + 8x = 11x)
Unlike Terms Different variables OR same variable with different exponents 4x and 4y
3x and 3x²		 NO!
(Keep them separate)
