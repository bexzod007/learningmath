Distributing with Negative Numbers

When distributing a negative multiplier, you must flip the sign of every term inside the parentheses. This is the most common area for computational errors in algebra.

Core Sign Rules:

  • Negative $\cdot$ Positive = Negative ($-\cdot + = -$)
  • Negative $\cdot$ Negative = Positive ($-\cdot - = +$)

Example 1: Single Negative Factor

Simplify: $-3(2x + 4)$

  • $-3 \cdot 2x = -6x$
  • $-3 \cdot (+4) = -12$
  • Result: $-6x - 12$

Example 2: Double Negative Operations

Simplify: $-4(3m - 5)$

  • $-4 \cdot 3m = -12m$
  • $-4 \cdot (-5) = +20$
  • Result: $-12m + 20$

Example 3: The Invisible "-1" Multiplier

When you see a simple negative sign in front of parentheses like $-(x - 7)$, it represents $-1 \cdot (x - 7)$:

  • $-1 \cdot x = -x$
  • $-1 \cdot (-7) = +7$
  • Result: $-x + 7$
