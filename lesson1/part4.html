Negative numbers

Introducing Negative Numbers

So far, we have only worked with numbers starting from zero and moving upwards. Now, we introduce numbers below zero, which are called negative numbers.

The easiest way to visualize negative numbers is by using a number line:

← negative                                  positive →
-3   -2   -1    0    1    2    3

Every number has a specific direction relative to zero:

  • Numbers to the left of 0 are negative (always written with a minus sign).
  • Numbers to the right of 0 are positive (greater than zero).

Examples:

  • -5 → negative five
  • -2 → negative two
  • +7 → positive seven

By convention, we usually don't write the + sign for positive numbers. If a number has no sign in front of it, it is automatically assumed to be positive:

+7 = 7

Real-World Examples of Negative Numbers

Negative numbers are not just abstract math—we use them every day to express values that drop below a starting baseline of zero:

  • Temperature: -5°C means five degrees below freezing (zero).
  • Bank Accounts: A balance of -$50 means an account is overdrawn and you owe money.
  • Elevation: -10 meters describes depth below sea level.
💡 Pro Tip: Zero (0) is neutral!
It is neither positive nor negative; it serves as the dividing line between the two on a number line.
