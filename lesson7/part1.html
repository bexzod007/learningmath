What is the Distributive Property?

The Distributive Property (or Distributive Law) is one of the most frequently used algebraic properties. It states that multiplying a number by a group of terms added or subtracted inside parentheses is equal to multiplying each term individually and then combining the results.

Algebraic Rule: $a(b + c) = ab + ac$  |  $a(b - c) = ab - ac$

Why does it work? Think of a real-world scenario:

Suppose you buy 3 school kits. Each kit contains 1 notebook ($x) and 1 pen ($2).

  • Method 1 (Grouped): Calculate the cost of one kit first, then multiply by 3: $3(x + 2)$
  • Method 2 (Distributed): Pay for 3 notebooks and 3 pens separately: $3 \cdot x + 3 \cdot 2 = 3x + 6$

Both methods yield the exact same financial cost, proving that $3(x + 2) = 3x + 6$.
