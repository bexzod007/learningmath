Using Letters for Numbers

1. The Core Idea: Upgrading Your Math Code

In elementary school, you solved problems with empty shapes: □ + 5 = 9.

In algebra, we simply replace the empty shape □ with a letter.

  • Arithmetic style: □ + 5 = 9
  • Algebra style: x + 5 = 9

Letters are just shorter, cleaner shortcuts to represent numbers.

2. Hidden Rules of Algebra

  1. The Invisible Multiplication Sign
    In arithmetic, you write: 3 × x.
    In algebra, we write: 3x.
    Putting a number right next to a letter means multiplication.
    Why? Because the multiplication symbol × looks too much like the letter x!
  2. The Invisible Number 1
    If a letter stands completely alone (like y), it actually has a hidden 1 in front of it.
    y is the exact same thing as 1y.
    -x is the exact same thing as -1x.

3. Real-World Example: Fast Food Order

Imagine ordering at a restaurant where a burger costs $5 (b) and fries cost $2 (f).

Your total bill can be written as: 5b + 2f.

If you buy 2 burgers (b = 2) and 3 fries (f = 3):

5(2) + 2(3) = 10 + 6 = $16

4. Summary & Definition

Definition: Using letters for numbers is the algebraic notation method where variables (like a, b, x) act as placeholders for numeric values in math expressions.
